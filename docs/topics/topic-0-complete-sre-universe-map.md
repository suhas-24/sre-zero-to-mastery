## Why These Layers Exist

- Layer 1 matters because SRE breaks instantly if Linux, networking, and automation feel like magic.
- Layer 2 matters because this is where reliability becomes an engineering discipline instead of reactive firefighting.
- Layer 3 matters because you cannot fix what you cannot see.
- Layer 4 matters because incidents are where theory meets stress, ambiguity, and business impact.
- Layer 5 matters because modern production systems live on cloud and container platforms.
- Layer 6 matters because bad delivery systems create reliability incidents faster than good engineers can repair them.
- Layer 7 matters because resilience that is never tested is mostly wishful thinking.
- Layer 8 matters because overload is one of the most common ways production systems fail.
- Layer 9 matters because a security failure is also a reliability failure.
- Layer 10 matters because the field is shifting toward platforms, cost-awareness, AI assistance, and cross-cloud complexity.
- Layer 11 matters because senior SRE work is never only technical.

## What Breaks If You Skip Foundations

- Skip Linux, and host-level failures look random.
- Skip networking, and every timeout feels mysterious.
- Skip automation, and toil quietly consumes the team.
- Skip SLOs, and reliability becomes opinion instead of policy.
- Skip observability, and incidents become guesswork.
- Skip incident management, and outage response turns chaotic.
- Skip cloud and Kubernetes depth, and modern infrastructure remains a black box.
- Skip soft skills, and technical correctness still fails to create trust.

## What Matters Most For Getting Hired In 2025-2026

- Linux, networking, cloud fundamentals
- Kubernetes and containers
- Terraform and infrastructure as code
- Observability: Prometheus, Grafana, OpenTelemetry
- Incident response and postmortem literacy
- SLO thinking and practical reliability tradeoffs
- Python, Bash, and increasingly Go
- Platform engineering awareness

## Where AI Agents Are Making The Biggest Impact Right Now

- alert deduplication and summarization
- incident timeline drafting
- runbook generation and lookup
- log and trace triage assistance
- change-risk analysis before deploys
- postmortem first-draft generation

The strongest pattern right now is augmentation, not replacement. Teams with good telemetry, clean ownership, and structured runbooks get the most value from AI-assisted SRE.
